Question 2

Work-centre flow allocation

What we do today — it's three mechanisms stacked, and none of them is a share

Worth being precise about this, because "hard-coded product splits" describes only the first of the three.

Layer 1 — the choice set is hard-coded per product family, in the loop that writes ProductionPolicies. Each family is allowed a different subset of the three unload methods:

FamilyUnload methods it is allowedWhere that is decided
*_INTERSTATE_*ULD, LONGREACHchain-2 notebook, production-policies cell — if a in ("UNLOAD_ULD", "UNLOAD_LONGREACH")
*_VIC_*ULD, HANDsame cell — if a in ("UNLOAD_ULD", "UNLOAD_HAND")
pickup (chain 1)ULD, LONGREACH, HANDall three emitted as competing recipes

That is a hard-coded eligibility rule with no number attached. It says interstate freight never arrives loose, which is a real operational claim — but it is buried in an if, not in a factors file, and nobody has signed it off.

Layer 2 — capacity comes from machine rates, not from a share. Each hub gets one notional machine of each type, scaled by a per-hub dock factor sized to that hub's own workload:

capacity(site, method) = rate_hr[method] × HUB_DOCK_SCALE[site] × 13.5 h

  machine_rates.csv          rate_hr   $/EA    fixed $/yr
  UNLOAD_ULD                   2,750   0.015     250,000
  UNLOAD_LONGREACH             1,750   0.020     150,000
  UNLOAD_HAND                    750   0.050      50,000

  Melbourne Parcel, scale ×3.493 →  ULD 129,652 · LR 82,505 · HAND 35,359 EA/day
  The scale is shared, so the ratio is ALWAYS 2750 : 1750 : 750 = 52.4% : 33.3% : 14.3%

So the capacity mix at every hub is identical and is a property of the assumed machine fleet — one of each. It is not a statement about how freight actually presents.

Layer 3 — the solver picks the cheapest allowed recipe until something binds. ULD is $0.015, long-reach $0.020, hand $0.050. With capacity nowhere near binding, the outcome is degenerate:

The problem in one sentence

We size docks from an assumed workload, the solver then fills the cheapest dock first, and we read the resulting split back out and report it as a finding. Hand unload comes out at exactly zero at both hubs — so the model currently books no hand-unload labour at all, which is the most expensive activity per parcel and certainly not zero in the real operation.

What Optilogic's construct does instead

Four UserDefinedVariables rows and two UserDefinedConstraints rows. Decoded:

UserDefinedVariables.csv — two terms make one variable
variablename                termname             coefficient  type        processname          groupbehavior
IN-HUB_UnloadingSplit_LR    TotalInboundVolume          -0.5  Production  grp_in-Hub_Unload    Aggregate
IN-HUB_UnloadingSplit_LR    Total_Unloading_LR          +1.0  Production  HUB_Unload_LR        Enumerate

UserDefinedConstraints.csv — pin the variable to zero
constraintname              variablename                constrainttype           constraintvalue
HubUnloading_Forced_LR      IN-HUB_UnloadingSplit_LR    Fixed With Tolerance     0

which is:   1·LR − 0.5·(LR + Other) = 0    →    LR = 50% of total unloading

The mechanism worth understanding is the group-behaviour pair. Aggregate on a process group sums every process in it into one term — that's the denominator. Enumerate on a single process picks out one — that's the numerator. Put a coefficient of −share on the denominator and +1 on the numerator, pin the whole thing to zero, and you have a ratio constraint.

Side by side

Now

  • Mix is an output — you find out what it was after the solve.
  • Changing it means changing capacities or costs, which changes other answers too.
  • Eligibility rules live in if statements in a notebook.
  • Cannot express "long reach handles 30% of arrivals".
  • Hand unload is structurally zero.

With user-defined constraints

  • Mix is an input — a number in a factors file we can show ops.
  • Varying it is a scenario item, not a rebuild.
  • All three methods stay eligible everywhere; the constraint does the work.
  • A band (not a pin) also tells you which way the mix wants to move.
  • Hand-unload labour is booked and costed.

The rows we would write

Our processes are already named <site>_<activity>, so the groups fall out of the existing naming with no new convention. For one hub, three methods, as a band rather than a pin:

Groups.csv — one process group per site (denominator)
grp_UNLOAD_Melbourne_Parcel,Processes,Melbourne_Parcel_UNLOAD_ULD,Include
grp_UNLOAD_Melbourne_Parcel,Processes,Melbourne_Parcel_UNLOAD_LONGREACH,Include
grp_UNLOAD_Melbourne_Parcel,Processes,Melbourne_Parcel_UNLOAD_HAND,Include

UserDefinedVariables.csv — two rows per method (target share 0.30 for long reach)
MPF_UnloadMix_LONGREACH,Total,   -0.30,Production,HUB_Melbourne_Parcel,grp_UNLOAD_Melbourne_Parcel,Aggregate
MPF_UnloadMix_LONGREACH,ThisOne, +1.00,Production,HUB_Melbourne_Parcel,Melbourne_Parcel_UNLOAD_LONGREACH,Enumerate

UserDefinedConstraints.csv — a BAND, not an equality: 30% ±5pp
MPF_UnloadMix_LONGREACH_lo,MPF_UnloadMix_LONGREACH,Min,-0.05,Include
MPF_UnloadMix_LONGREACH_hi,MPF_UnloadMix_LONGREACH,Max, 0.05,Include

Because the variable is already actual − target×total, bounding it at ±0.05 of the total is the band. Repeat for ULD and hand and you have one site. Eight sort sites × three methods × (2 variable rows + 2 constraint rows) is 96 rows across two new tables — all generated, none hand-maintained. If facilitynamegroupbehavior = Enumerate against a facility group does what we think, it collapses to about 12 rows; that is the first thing to test.

Set two of the three, never all three

Three shares that must sum to 1 plus a flow balance is one constraint too many — pin ULD and long reach, let hand take the residue. Constraining all three means any rounding error makes the model infeasible with no diagnostic. Their prototype constrains both of its two methods, which is already one too many; it survives because both are exactly 0.5.

It needs site_machines and procs_at, both of which that cell already builds, so it slots in directly after it. It emits three tables: the process groups appended to Groups, plus UserDefinedVariables and UserDefinedConstraints.

3 · Relax the eligibility rules at the same time

The constraint only means something if all three methods are actually available. In the production-policies cell, the two family filters have to open up — otherwise you are constraining a share of a choice set that was already forced.

- if a in ("UNLOAD_ULD", "UNLOAD_LONGREACH"):        # interstate
- if a in ("UNLOAD_ULD", "UNLOAD_HAND"):             # Victoria
+ if a in UNLOADS:                                   # all three; the UDC sets the mix

If we believe interstate genuinely never arrives loose, that belief should move into unload_mix.csv as a per-family share of zero, where it is visible — not stay in an if.

What to check on the first run

Hand unload should stop being zero, and total handling cost should rise — we are forcing volume off the cheapest machine onto dearer ones. If it doesn't rise, the constraint isn't binding and something is wrong with the group scoping. Then watch for the failure mode we have hit before: a pinned share above what a capacity allows goes infeasible rather than spilling, which is why WC_MIX_MODE=band is the default and pin is opt-in.

Question 1

The sort bypass — how theirs works, and what ours is missing

Four devices, and they are easy to conflate

"Bypass" in their file is not one thing. It is four separate mechanisms that happen to appear together, and knowing which is which is most of the answer.

DeviceWhere it livesWhat it does
A · The depth tagProducts — the _L1_ / _L2_ / _L3_ in a product name Carries how finely this parcel has already been sorted along with the parcel. A downstream site reads the tag to know whether it still has work to do. This is the memory of the network.
B · Competing sort recipesProductionPolicies — same input, same work centre, three outputs at three prices At the hub, Unload_Parcel_1_L1_Sort can become Sort_Parcel_1 ($1, regular), Sort_Parcel_2 ($2, depot-level) or Outbound-Parcel_1 ($3, deep). Buying a deeper sort costs more, here, now.
C · The free conversionProductionPolicies — a row with no work centre and no process At the downstream site, a deep-sorted arrival becomes despatchable without touching a sorter. The parcel still pays unload and reload — it is physically there — it just consumes no sortation capacity. This is cross-dock.
D · The ByPass modeTransportationModes + a lane using it A named mode on the hub → depot lane so the direct movement is priced and reported separately, and can be switched off by scenario. This is the one that skips the building rather than the sorter.
C and D are different savings

A free conversion saves one sort. The ByPass mode saves a whole facility visit — unload, sort, reload, and a linehaul leg. Their prototype demonstrates C properly and leaves D broken (the lane carries products the depot has no recipe for). We have built D and not C. Worth agreeing the vocabulary before the next call.

Their three configurations, walked end to end

ConfigurationAt the hubAt the sort hubAt the depotSorts

the only one enabled		 Sort_Parcel_1 · $1 unload → Sort_Parcel_2_2 · $1 unload → Outbound-Parcel_3 · $13

The parcel visits all three sites in every configuration. What changes is how many of them run a sorter. And the thing that stops the model choosing "deep" every time is that deep costs $3 where regular costs $1 — on the same work centre. That price is the whole economic story: a deeper sort means more chutes, smaller batches and a slower line, so it costs more per parcel at the site that does it, and saves a sort downstream. The model trades those off.

What chain 2 already has

We built a bypass in Change 25 from their earlier demo, and it is on (INTERSTATE_BYPASS = free). Chain 2 currently supports three routings:

RoutingLegLane rowsProduct pathHow it is controlled
Two rounds6 → 742 + 435 Sorted → Despatch1_<hub> → <hub>_Unloaded2 → <hub>_Sorted2 → <hub>_Despatch2 only the site pairs the scans observed, each banded ±5%
Bypass (Change 25)7c510 Sorted → <hub>_Despatch2 — a second load recipe at the arrival hub competing recipe, cost decides, share banded ±5%
Cross-dock (12b)6x12 Unloaded → XDock_<hub> → sorted at the receiving depot pinned to measured EA ±5%, four lanes out of MPF

Note what the cross-dock row is: it is the hub declining to sort and pushing the work downstream. It is the mirror image of a deep sort, and we already have the mechanic — XDock products move unsorted and the depot's sorter picks them up.

The three gaps

1 · We have no depth tag, so the middle configuration is unbuildable

Our Sorted is a single state. A parcel is either sorted or it isn't; there is no record of how deeply. So the bypass decision has to be taken at the load step and it is all-or-nothing about the second site:

two competing load recipes at the arrival hub, both consuming <c>_INTERSTATE_Sorted
BOM_LOAD_INT_<c>      →  <c>_INTERSTATE_Despatch1_<hub>   leg 6  → second site, gets sorted there
BOM_LOAD_DIRECT_<c>   →  <c>_INTERSTATE_<hub>_Despatch2   leg 7c → straight to the delivering PDC

We can say "skip the second site" and "sort at the second site". We cannot say "pass through the second site without being sorted there" — their configuration 2, and the one the tentacle scenario needs, because a parcel that overflows Melbourne Parcel's deep-sort capacity still has to travel via somewhere.

2 · A deep sort costs us exactly what a regular sort costs

SORT_AUTO_LGE is $0.01/EA and 202,500 EA/day at Melbourne Parcel whether it produces 3 buckets or 30. So bypassing is strictly cheaper — it saves three machine touches and a linehaul leg and costs nothing extra. Left to economics alone, the solver bypasses everything.

3 · Which is why the shares are held by measurement, not by physics

There are 22 pairs of Min/Max flow constraints in the build doing exactly that job:

MPF  PP  single-sort share held to 58.0% ±5%   (Min 14,440 / Max 17,164 EA)
TPF  PP  single-sort share held to 77.4% ±5%   (Min  7,739 / Max  8,808 EA)
SWP  PP  single-sort share held to 86.2% ±5%   (Min  4,086 / Max  4,590 EA)
MGF  EP  single-sort share held to 70.0% ±5%   (Min  1,980 / Max  2,285 EA)   … and 18 more

Those bands are standing in for a constraint the model does not have. Remove them and the answer is 100% bypass, everywhere, immediately. That is fine for calibration — we are reproducing what the scans measured — but it means the bypass share is currently an input, not a result, and no scenario can move it for a reason.

And one thing that is already broken

For the interstate family the two-round branch is orphaned: *_INTERSTATE_*_Unloaded2 and *_INTERSTATE_*_Sorted2 have no production policy at all, and no lane on leg 6 carries an interstate Despatch1. The observed routing filter found no interstate second sorts, so it pruned the recipes and left the products behind. Interstate is therefore at 100% bypass structurally, not by cost — the competing recipe exists but its downstream is missing. Anyone reading the tables would reasonably think there is a choice there.

Applying their pattern to chain 2

The minimum useful version is one new depth level, not three. Our chain has at most two hub sorts, so "deep" only has to mean "needs no second hub sort".

TodayUnloaded → Sorted → Despatch1_<hub> → Unloaded2 → Sorted2 → Despatch2
ProposedUnloaded → Sorted_R → Despatch1_<hub>_R → Unloaded2_R → SORT → Sorted2 → Despatch2
Unloaded → Sorted_D → Despatch1_<hub>_D → Unloaded2_D → free conversion → Sorted2 → Despatch2
UnchangedUnloaded → Sorted_D → Despatch2 direct (leg 7c) — the Change-25 bypass still skips the site entirely

Both depth flavours rejoin at Sorted2, so everything downstream — despatch products, leg 7, the PDCs, demand — is untouched. The two branches differ only in whether the second site's sorter is consumed.

The cleanest way to price it — and it needs nothing from Optilogic

Rather than inventing a new constraint type for the tentacle limit, model the deep sort as a second, slower process on the same work centre. A deeper sort really is slower per parcel — more chutes, smaller batches. So:

Processes.csv
  Melbourne_Parcel_SORT_AUTO_LGE        WC_SORT_AUTO_LGE_Melbourne_Parcel   202,500/day   $0.010
  Melbourne_Parcel_SORT_AUTO_LGE_DEEP   WC_SORT_AUTO_LGE_Melbourne_Parcel   140,000/day   $0.018
  Same work centre, so they share ONE capacity. Deep sorting consumes it faster.

Then the tentacle constraint is just the work-centre capacity, already in the model. A site that deep-sorts for too many destinations runs out of sorter before it runs out of parcels, and the excess is pushed onto the regular recipe and a second sort elsewhere — which is precisely the behaviour Optilogic described. No new constraint type, no binaries, no MIP.

The rate and cost premium are the two numbers to get from ops: how much slower is a deep sort, and what does it cost per parcel. Both go in machine_rates.csv next to the existing figures, flagged as placeholders until confirmed. Once they are real, the ±5% calibration bands can come off interstate and the bypass share becomes an output — a testable prediction against the measured 90% single-sort rather than an assumption reproducing it.

Sequence, and what to check
  • Clear the orphaned interstate round-2 products first, or a genuinely broken deep branch will be indistinguishable from the pruned one already sitting there.
  • Build it off first (DEEP_SORT = off emitting nothing) and confirm a byte-identical rebuild, the same way Change 24/25 were introduced.
  • Turn it on with the bands still in place — the solve should barely move, because the bands pin the shares. Then take the bands off interstate and see whether the capacity-and-cost story reproduces the measured 90% single sort on its own. That is the falsifiable test.

What to put to Optilogic

  • Does a production- or flow-count constraint count distinct destinations, or only volume? If only volume, the slower-process approach above is strictly better and we should agree on it rather than wait.
  • Two processes on one work centre sharing its capacity — confirm NEO treats it as one pooled capacity and not two independent ones. Everything above depends on that.
  • Their configuration 2 puts a free conversion at every downstream site. Ours would put it at one. Is there any reason a deep-sorted parcel would need converting more than once?
  • Fix the ByPass-mode lane in the prototype so we can see device D working as intended — it is the one we have already built and would like to compare against.

The deep sort is structural: build it defaulting to off for a byte-identical rebuild, then turn it on with the calibration bands still holding, then take the bands off interstate and see whether cost and capacity reproduce the measured single-sort share on their own.

The two are connected. The deep-sort premium and the unload mix are both numbers we currently do not state — one is buried in a capacity ratio, the other in the absence of a price difference — and both are exactly the kind of parameter the workbook exists to put in front of ops.
