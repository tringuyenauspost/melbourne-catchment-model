How a postcode gets its share of pickup volume

The model used to give every postcode a site collects from exactly the same volume. It now splits that volume by how often the vans and trucks actually stop there. This is how that percentage is worked out, end to end.

Measured over Mon–Fri 18–22 May 2026 Source CCP booking extract Covers {{n_sites}} collection sites, {{n_cells}} postcodes

What changed, in one line

  • Before: Melbourne Transport collects from {{mt_cells}} postcodes, so each one was modelled as collecting {{mt_ea}} ÷ {{mt_cells}} = {{mt_equal}} parcels a day — the same number for the CBD fringe as for a single weekly stop in Whittlesea.
  • Now: each postcode gets the share of the site's volume that matches its share of the site's collection stops.
  • The site total does not move. Melbourne Transport still collects {{mt_ea}} parcels a day. Only the split between its postcodes changes.

1Start with every stop a route made

  • The CCP export lists one row per job — one vehicle stopping at one address on one day, on a named route.
  • We take one clean working week: Monday 18 to Friday 22 May 2026. The extract also holds Saturday, Sunday and a second Monday; including the second Monday would count that weekday twice.
  • That gives {{all_stops}} collection stops across the seven sites.

2Keep only the stops that collect from the public

Not every stop is a collection, and what counts differs between the two kinds of site. This is deliberate, not an inconsistency:

  • Both kinds of site keep customer pickups, and keep the places the public lodges — post offices, retail outlets, parcel lockers and street posting boxes. Someone lodging at one of our counters is freight entering the network just as much as a collection from their loading dock.
  • The two transport facilities (Melbourne Transport, Dandenong Transport) additionally drop their other network stops, because those are at our own buildings: Melbourne Parcel Facility, Dandenong Letters Centre, Tullamarine. The booking instructions say so outright — "Collect all for Mulgrave PDC", "Collect for Darebin PDC" — they name the building the freight is going to, and {{empties}} of them are collecting empty equipment rather than parcels. That is movement between our own sites, not freight coming in from outside.
  • The five PDC van operations (Sunshine West, Melbourne North, Bayswater, Oakleigh South, Dandenong South) collect most of that lodgement: post office and retail-outlet stops alone are {{rv_lodge}} of them. The two transports clear a further {{tr_lodge}} on their country runs, at Abbotsford, Romsey, Lancefield, Woodend, Gisborne, Cockatoo and Gembrook.
  • Drop stops at our own buildings in both cases — a van calling at its own depot or another delivery centre is returning or transferring, not collecting. That removes {{own_stops}} stops.
  • Drop delivery-only bookings. A stop that only drops parcels off collects nothing.

  • {{n_halve}} of the {{n_cells}} postcodes more than halve; {{n_double}} more than double.

Postcodes we removed, and why

{{n_drop_word}} of the {{n_poly}} postcode–site pairs collect nothing from the public and were dropped rather than given a token volume:

  • Eight are delivery-only — the van goes there, but only ever to drop parcels off. They were in the catchment because the original map was drawn without filtering on booking type.
  • Eight only ever see our own buildings — the site's only visit to that postcode all week is to another site's dock. Oakleigh South's only stop in 3175 is Dandenong delivery centre; Sunshine West's only stop in 3122 is Hawthorn.
  • No volume is lost when they go. The shares are worked out within each site, so the volume simply moves to that site's remaining postcodes.

What this deliberately does not change

The weights are a shape, not a level.

  • Total pickup is unchanged at {{metro_ea}} parcels a day across the seven sites.
  • Every site's own total is unchanged. All seven still collect exactly what the 2025 peak and the planning factor say they collect.
  • No building's workload changes, so sorter sizing, work-centre capacity and the facility throughput caps are all untouched — confirmed identical in the rebuilt model.
  • The only thing that moves is how much each postcode contributes, and therefore how many vehicle trips are costed on each collection lane.

How we know the shares are about right

  • The booking system records an actual parcel count on some stops — but only at the two transport facilities. The five van sites record none at all, so stop counts are the only signal available there.
  • Where we can check, counting stops predicts the recorded volume closely: a correlation of {{r_mt}} at Melbourne Transport and {{r_dt}} at Dandenong Transport between a postcode's share of stops and its share of recorded parcels.
  • The recorded parcel counts are far too sparse to use as the volume itself — they total about {{ccp_day}} a day against the model's {{metro_ea}}, under 1%. They can tell us the shape is right; they cannot set the level.
